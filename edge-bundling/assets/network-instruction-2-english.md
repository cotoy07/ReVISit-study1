# Research Overview: Evaluation of Network Diagram Readability Before and After Edge Bundling

## 1. Background
A network diagram is a visual representation of connections between things or people, composed of "points (nodes)" and "connecting lines (edges)." It is widely used across various fields to understand relationships within data.

However, as the scale of data grows, the diagram can become cluttered, making it difficult to read. To address this, a technique called "**edge bundling**" is often used to bundle multiple lines together, reducing visual complexity.

On the other hand, edge bundling can sometimes have the opposite effect depending on the specific task, meaning it is not necessarily useful in all situations.

## 2. Research Objective
The objective of this study is to analyze under which combinations of conditions edge bundling provides high readability and to clarify the optimal combinations. The factors being analyzed include:
- Network type
- Density and scale
- User tasks

In the questionnaire for this study, participants will evaluate the **readability** of network diagrams before and after edge bundling based on the following three criteria.

### Definition of Readability
1. **Ease of Information Retrieval**: Is it easy to grasp relationships and the overall structure (such as connections between groups)?
2. **Visual Clarity**: Are the nodes and edges easy to distinguish? Can specific lines be tracked accurately?
3. **Aesthetic Appeal**: Is the diagram visually pleasing or comfortable to look at?

## 3. Experimental Procedure
The experiment will be conducted according to the following steps:

1. **Comparison**: Compare the two network diagrams presented on the left and right sides of the screen (before edge bundling and after edge bundling).
2. **Selection**: For each of the three criteria mentioned above, select "which one is better (or if they are equal)."
3. **Trials**: A total of **63 sets** of comparisons will be performed.
- **Estimated Time Required**: Approximately 30 minutes
